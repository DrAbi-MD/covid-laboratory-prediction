Comparison with the Published Study
This reproduction used the COVID-specific feature set (34 predictors) from the 1,624-patient OSR development cohort; therefore, comparisons are made primarily with the COVID-specific models reported by Cabitza et al.
Overall, the reproduced development models showed performance broadly comparable with the published findings. Logistic Regression achieved an AUC of 0.877 compared with 0.83 in the publication, with accuracy 0.800 vs 0.83, sensitivity 0.809 vs 0.85, specificity 0.792 vs 0.82, and Brier score 0.142 vs 0.14. Random Forest achieved an AUC of 0.881 vs 0.84, accuracy 0.806 vs 0.84, sensitivity 0.796 vs 0.84, specificity 0.816 vs 0.84, and Brier score 0.141 vs 0.12. Thus, reproduced AUCs were slightly higher, while threshold-based performance measures were modestly lower but of similar magnitude.
During internal-external validation, reproduced mean AUCs were 0.895 for Logistic Regression and 0.836 for Random Forest. Random Forest showed particularly high specificity (0.960) but substantially lower sensitivity (0.393), indicating a difference in classification behaviour at the 0.50 threshold despite preserved discrimination.
On the 54-patient historical external cohort, Logistic Regression achieved 90.7% specificity (49/54) and Random Forest 98.1% specificity (53/54). Because all historical cases were COVID-negative, sensitivity and ROC-AUC could not be estimated.
Overall, the reproduction demonstrates broad agreement with the published performance of the COVID-specific models, particularly during model development and historical negative-control validation. Differences were most evident during internal-external validation and may reflect unavailable details of the original hyperparameter search, random data partitioning, SMOTE implementation, preprocessing, calibration, and software versions. These differences are retained and reported as part of the reproducibility analysis rather than retrospectively modifying the analytical approach to improve agreement.
